# Discovery Brief

## Discovery Context

The Traduce Señas project arises from the identification of communication barriers between deaf or hard-of-hearing people and hearing people who do not know sign language.

The SRS identifies the limited availability of interpreters and the general lack of knowledge of sign language as important factors, particularly in everyday situations, educational institutions, workplaces, public services, and other interaction contexts.

## Identified Findings

### Finding 1 — Communication Barrier

Deaf or hard-of-hearing people may experience difficulties communicating with hearing people who do not know sign language.

### Finding 2 — Limited Availability of Interpreters

Professional interpreters are an alternative for facilitating communication, but their availability is limited, particularly in rural areas, public institutions, and everyday situations.

### Finding 3 — Need for an Accessible Alternative

There is a need for a technological tool that can recognize signs and provide understandable translations for hearing people.

### Finding 4 — Everyday Use

The solution should focus on everyday situations such as education, work, commercial establishments, and requesting services.

### Finding 5 — Ease of Use

The application should be accessible, intuitive, and easy to use, including for people who do not have previous knowledge of sign language.

## Assumptions

### Assumption 1 — User Interest

It is assumed that both hearing people and deaf people will be willing to use an application that facilitates communication between both communities.

**Status:** To be validated.

### Assumption 2 — Device Access

It is assumed that users will have access to smartphones or computers with cameras to use the application.

**Status:** To be validated.

### Assumption 3 — Internet Access

It is assumed that users will have access to an internet connection because the complete functionality of the system requires connectivity.

**Status:** To be validated.

### Assumption 4 — Translation Validation

It is expected that certified interpreters will participate in validating translations and providing accurate data to improve the system.

**Status:** To be validated.

### Assumption 5 — System Updates

It is assumed that the system can be continuously updated to incorporate new signs and improve recognition accuracy.

**Status:** To be validated.

## Research Conducted

The SRS used as the source for this document presents the project's problem, users, needs, and assumptions. However, it does not document formal interviews, surveys, or direct observations with users.

Therefore, no user research results are presented as confirmed facts. The findings above are derived from the analysis of the SRS, while the assumptions require validation during the following stages of the project.

## Conclusions

The analysis indicates a need to facilitate communication between deaf or hard-of-hearing people and hearing people.

The proposed solution should prioritize accessibility, ease of use, and real-time sign translation.

The assumptions related to user acceptance, device availability, connectivity, and translation accuracy should be validated in future stages of the project.
