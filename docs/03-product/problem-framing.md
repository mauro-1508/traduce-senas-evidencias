# Problem Framing

## The problem

**Who has it?**

Deaf or hard-of-hearing people face communication difficulties when interacting with hearing people who do not know sign language. Hearing people who need to communicate with sign language users but do not have the necessary knowledge are also affected.

**What problem do they have?**

There is a communication barrier mainly caused by the general lack of knowledge of sign language and the limited availability of professional interpreters. This makes it difficult for deaf people to communicate autonomously with hearing people in different everyday situations.

**When does it occur?**

The problem occurs mainly in everyday situations where a deaf person needs to communicate with a hearing person who does not know sign language, such as in educational institutions, workplaces, public services, commercial establishments, healthcare services, or when requesting a service.

**What is the impact?**

Communication difficulties can limit the autonomy, social participation, and integration of deaf or hard-of-hearing people. In addition, the limited availability of interpreters, especially in rural areas, public institutions, and everyday situations, makes timely and effective communication more difficult.

**How do they solve it today?**

Currently, people can rely on professional sign language interpreters or attempt to communicate through other means. However, interpreters are not always available, especially in everyday situations. Therefore, these alternatives do not always provide an immediate and accessible solution.

## Why it is worth solving

Solving this problem is important because it can help reduce communication barriers between deaf or hard-of-hearing people and hearing people.

Traduce Señas aims to provide a technological alternative through a mobile application capable of recognizing signs using the device's camera and translating them into text or audio in real time.

The solution seeks to facilitate communication in everyday situations, promote the autonomy of people with hearing disabilities, and support their participation in educational, professional, healthcare, and public service environments.

Additionally, the project aims to contribute to a more inclusive and accessible society by partially reducing dependence on the availability of professional interpreters.
